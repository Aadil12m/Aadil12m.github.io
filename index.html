Autonomous Warehouse AMR

Scans every rack in a warehouse and builds a complete map of them.

Illustration: the robot drives the aisles, maps the walls with LiDAR and marks each rack bay once it scans it.

From laser to wheels

  1. 2D LiDARScans the aisle and feeds SLAM Toolbox and Nav2
  2. SLAM Toolbox, EKFBuild the map and work out where the robot is
  3. Nav2Plans the path down each aisle
  4. ESP32Low-level motor control
  5. RMCS-2303 driversTake Modbus ASCII commands and turn the mecanum wheels

I built the robot and its software. It drives the aisles on LiDAR-based autonomy, scans each rack it passes and stitches the scans into a map of the warehouse's racks. I also built a web interface for it.

An ESP32 drives the four mecanum wheels through RMCS-2303 motor drivers over Modbus ASCII. The angled rollers let the base strafe into an aisle without turning. Most of the code is Python, with some C++. Nav2, SLAM Toolbox and an EKF handle navigation and localization, and I fried more than one motor driver during bring-up.

Reconfigurable Piezo-Jet Robot

Six hinged jet modules that fold into a box to swim and open up to hunt.

Drive it yourself. Hold W A S D to swim and Q or E to rise and sink. Line up under a sinking fish, press R to open into hunting form, then R again to fold and trap it. The robot can only swim while folded, and it plays on its own when you leave it alone.

With a small team under a professor, I'm recreating the reconfigurable robot from Zhang et al. (2025, Sensors & Actuators A, vol. 388). Six MPJDU modules, each driven by a piezo disc, connect through hinges. The paper's robot opens into a hunting form to trap small organisms and folds into a box, its submerged form, to carry them. Firing the module on one face pushes the box the other way, so it can move along any axis.

We're designing a better signal generator and amplifier to drive the piezos. A BNO086 IMU on board lets us measure the thrust from each module on its own.

Elephant-Trunk End Effector

A soft, biomimetic gripper modeled on the finger-like tip of an elephant's trunk.

Illustration: one chamber inflates to bend the soft body, and two finger-like tips curl around an egg to lift it.

I worked in a soft robotics lab on the design of an end effector that copies the way an elephant's trunk tip pinches and holds objects with its finger-like extensions. A soft gripper bends by inflating chambers in a silicone body, so it can wrap around fragile objects such as an egg.

On the vision side, I built a PyTorch pipeline that pairs YOLOv8-pose keypoint detection with Segment Anything, tuned for real-time inference on GPU.
